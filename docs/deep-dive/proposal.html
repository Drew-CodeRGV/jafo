JAFO — Build Your Own Passive Radio Observatory

Two afternoons. One Raspberry Pi. A working signals observatory you take home.
Drew Lentz · KE5ZJO · drew@drewlentz.com · jafo.live

Over two afternoons, every student builds a working JAFO: a Raspberry Pi kit (~$244) that passively listens to the local P25 public-safety radio system and the sky above it, turns what it hears into plain-English incidents with AI, and reports to a live shared site at jafo.live. Day 1 builds a P25 receiver by hand in GNU Radio, so nothing is a black box. Day 2 layers on the JAFO stack: one command that self-configures and joins the fleet, Claude turning calls into incidents and unit timelines, privacy and the law, and ADS-B + ACARS from the sky. Everyone goes home with a working station for their own community.

What every student leaves with

  • A working JAFO node they built and understand, the hardware kit to run it anywhere, and a GNU Radio / OP25 skillset to decode P25 by hand.
  • A working grasp of the AI layer: how radio calls become incidents, units and a timeline.
  • A clear answer to "is this legal, and what should I hide?"
  • Their node live on the jafo.live fleet, contributing to a shared dataset.

Why it fits WLPC

  • Hands-on, take-home hardware, the Deep Dive hallmark. Everyone walks out with a device they built.
  • Real RF fundamentals: SDR, antennas, GNU Radio and trunked-system decoding, from first principles, plus the sky: ADS-B aircraft and ACARS datalink sharing one radio.
  • Modern, practical AI: speech-to-text plus an LLM that links calls into incidents and tracks every unit's status, on a $244 kit, not a buzzword.
  • Responsible by design: a full module on privacy, redaction and the law, the questions everyone asks first about public-safety radio.
  • Community by design: every node joins a growing fleet; the more people build one, the better the shared picture.
What it needs from WLPC (instructor handles the rest): a dedicated SSID/VLAN or switch for the Pis; a shared outdoor/window-mounted 800 MHz antenna feed to the room (indoor reception is the one real risk; I bring a recorded-IQ fallback so every decode milestone lands); and standard bench power. I pre-scope the Phoenix system and pre-cache its RadioReference data.
