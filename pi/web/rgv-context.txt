### Region
The Lower Rio Grande Valley is the southernmost tip of Texas, along the
US-Mexico border. Two counties dominate radio traffic: Hidalgo County
(McAllen-Edinburg-Mission metro, pop ~870k) and Cameron County
(Brownsville-Harlingen metro, pop ~420k). The Rio Grande forms the
international border with Tamaulipas, Mexico.

### Radio Conventions & Common Misheard Phrases

**Phonetic alphabet** (NATO-modified, common in TX): Adam, Boy/Bravo,
Charles/Charlie, David/Delta, Edward/Echo, Frank/Foxtrot, George/Golf,
Henry/Hotel, Ida/India, John/Juliet, King/Kilo, Lincoln/Lima,
Mary/Mike, Nora/November, Ocean/Oscar, Paul/Papa, Queen/Quebec,
Robert/Romeo, Sam/Sierra, Tom/Tango, Union/Uniform, Victor, William/Whiskey,
X-ray, Young/Yankee, Zebra/Zulu.

**Ten-codes** widely used: 10-4 (OK / acknowledged), 10-6 (busy),
10-7 (out of service), 10-8 (in service), 10-9 (repeat), 10-10
(off-duty), 10-13 (weather/road conditions), 10-15 (prisoner in
custody), 10-19 (return to station), 10-20 (location), 10-22
(disregard), 10-33 (emergency / help), 10-50 (accident), 10-51 (wrecker),
10-54 (livestock), 10-55 (intoxicated driver), 10-79 (notify coroner),
10-99 (officer down).

**Common transcription confusions** to look for and correct:
- "central medical oven" → most likely "central medical center" or
  "central command" (Whisper hallucination of "oven")
- "DHR" sometimes transcribed as "the air" or "the H R"
- "MFE" / "McAllen Intl" / "the airport" — all the same place
- "VVICE" / "VV ICE" → "vice" (VICE squad)
- "ICE" alone is often US Immigration & Customs Enforcement
- "El Pez" / "El Paz" → likely a unit or location near La Paz
- "Code 4" → scene secure / no further assistance needed
- "Code 3" → lights & sirens, urgent response
- "Code 2" → urgent, no lights/sirens
- "Code 1" → routine
- "Mike X" / "M X" / "MX" → "Medic X" (EMS unit number)
- "Adam X" → patrol unit number (e.g., "Adam 12" = patrol unit 12)
- "Charlie X" → command-staff unit
- "the trauma center" / "the trauma" → typically DHR or Valley Baptist
- Spanish words (this is a heavily bilingual region): "el otro lado"
  (the other side, i.e., Mexico), "la línea" (the [border] line),
  "el río" (the river), "el bordo" (the levee)

**When in doubt**, prefer vague-but-correct over specific-but-wrong:
say "in McAllen" rather than name a wrong street; say "responding"
rather than name a wrong unit; say "a medical emergency" rather than
invent a hospital name. Mark unclear specifics with phrasing like
"a location described as X" or "what appeared to be a Y."
